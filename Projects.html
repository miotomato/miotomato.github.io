Multi-omics integration and statistical modeling of BRD4-mediated transcriptional condensates identifies transcription factors linked to clinical outcomes

Using statistical modeling and multi-omics integration, I analyzed BRD4 and H3K27ac ChIP-seq, ATAC-seq, and RNA-seq datasets across multiple conditions to identify candidate transcriptional regulators associated with BRD4-mediated, pH-sensitive transcriptional condensates in mouse macrophages. This analysis identified RELA, IRF family, and STAT family transcription factors as candidate regulators, and experimental validation confirmed that their binding patterns were aligned with BRD4 across pH conditions.

Using multivariable survival analysis, I further identified candidate transcriptional regulators associated with patient survival in colon cancer. The analysis suggested that putative transcriptional condensate activity in colon tumor cells was more likely to correlate with poor prognosis, whereas T cell-associated transcriptional condensate activity was more likely to be linked to better survival.

Large-scale ChIP-seq data integration and SQL-based genomic resource development for CTCF binding analysis

I built CTCFexplorer (www.ctcf.info), an SQL-backed, user-friendly genomic data resource and web platform that integrates more than 4,000 public human and mouse CTCF ChIP-seq datasets into a searchable database. The workflow begins with large-scale data collection from public repositories, metadata curation, data cleaning, quality control, and standardized ChIP-seq processing, followed by peak calling, integration of thousands of peak files, and statistical analysis to identify constitutive and cell type-specific CTCF binding events at genome scale. I then structured the processed data into SQL tables and developed a web-accessible database to support querying, visualization, data mining, and downstream biological interpretation of CTCF binding. The platform also includes GitHub-based CI/CD auto-deployment to support maintainable updates and reproducible delivery of the web resource.

Genomic clustering tendency of transcription factors reflects phase-separated transcriptional condensates at super-enhancers

I developed a statistical framework to quantify genome-wide clustering patterns of transcription factor motifs and binding organization. These clustering patterns are linked to transcriptional condensates at super-enhancers. Genomic regions with densely clustered transcription factor binding sites are more enriched at cell type-specific super-enhancers and exhibit higher chromatin accessibility, stronger chromatin interactions, and greater association with cancer outcomes.

Source: Nucleic Acids Research | GitHub

Topology-based machine learning analysis of chromatin structure for cell-type classification using multiplexed chromatin tracing data

I developed machine learning models to classify cell types from multiplexed chromatin tracing data, with a focus on feature design, model development, evaluation, and biological interpretation. I applied a topology-based representation of chromatin structure using Delaunay tessellation and constructed features from thousands of spatial topological units. Using these features, I trained and evaluated random forest classifiers on mouse cortex chromatin tracing data. The models achieved an overall accuracy of ~0.75 for distinguishing major cell types, compared with ~0.5 accuracy from random controls. Pairwise classification performance further revealed clustering patterns consistent with known major cortical cell classes.

In this project, I designed and implemented the full analysis pipeline, including data preprocessing and curation, coordinate imputation, feature engineering, machine learning model training, performance evaluation, and interpretation of results. This work demonstrates the feasibility of applying machine learning and topology-based feature design to extract biologically meaningful patterns from spatial chromatin organization data.
